# Westlake Corporation — Discovery Call Script
**Paste-ready input for LogiCov dev environment**
**Call date:** 16 September 2026
**Duration:** ~30 minutes
**Participants:** Relationship Manager (RM); Jonathan H. Baksht, Senior Vice President and Chief Financial Officer, Westlake Corporation; Head of Treasury, Westlake Corporation
**Purpose of the call:** Discuss the Company’s position after the 2025 trough, the second-quarter recovery, the April 2026 revolver, and what the bank group should underwrite on the $1.5 billion senior unsecured facility.

---

## The conversation

**RM:** Jonathan, thanks for making time. I have Treasury on as well. This is a discovery conversation, not a term-sheet pitch. We want the company as it sits after the 2025 trough and the April refinance, and what you want the bank group to do with the revolver. I’ll keep notes and come back with a structure that matches what you tell us today.

**CFO (Jonathan Baksht):** That is the right frame. I started as CFO on June 15. Steve Bender is still here as Special Advisor through year-end, so the file is continuous. Jean-Marc Gilson has been CEO since July 2024. Albert Chao is Executive Chairman. James Chao is Senior Chairman. Underwrite the operating company, not a family guarantee.

**RM:** Start with the request so we do not bury it. What do you want from the group?

**Head of Treasury:** The facility is already in place. Credit Agreement dated April 2, 2026. JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A. is administrative agent. $1.5 billion senior unsecured revolving credit facility, maturity April 2, 2031. It replaced the June 9, 2022 $1.5 billion revolver, which we terminated the same day. Same amount, five-year tenor, senior unsecured, no subsidiary guarantee. Purpose is general corporate purposes, letters of credit, and working capital. It is a liquidity backstop. We are not looking to draw it. It was undrawn at year-end 2025 and at March 31, 2026, and it is still undrawn.

**RM:** Pricing, sublimits, and the covenant as they sit today?

**Head of Treasury:** Borrowings are, at our option, Term SOFR plus a spread of 1.000 percent to 1.625 percent, or ABR plus 0.000 percent to 0.625 percent, in each case on a ratings grid. Undrawn commitment fee is 0.090 percent to 0.200 percent, same grid. At BBB / Baa2 we are inside that grid. Letter-of-credit sublimit is $150 million. Swingline is a discretionary $50 million. Accordion is up to $500 million, in increments of at least $25 million, if lenders agree. We are not asking to exercise the accordion.

Sole maintenance financial covenant is a quarterly total leverage ratio. S&P has described the predecessor test as net debt to EBITDA not more than 3.5 to 1.00. We are inside it. We are not opening the covenant.

**RM:** Walk me through the company a lender should underwrite. Two segments, as I read the 10-K.

**CFO:** Two segments. Housing and Infrastructure Products, HIP, and Performance and Essential Materials, PEM.

HIP sells pipe and fittings, siding, trim, window and door profiles, and related building products into North American residential construction and repair-and-remodel. 2025 net external sales were $4.148 billion — Housing Products $3.513 billion, Infrastructure Products $635 million. Operating income was $587 million. Adjusted EBITDA was $839 million. That was the earnings ballast in the trough.

PEM produces polyethylene, PVC resin, chlor-alkali — chlorine and caustic soda — epoxy, and related intermediates. Cost position is ethane-based Gulf Coast. 2025 net external sales were $7.022 billion — Performance Materials $4.018 billion, Essential Materials $3.004 billion. Operating loss was $2.100 billion. That number embeds Identified Items. Underlying PEM earnings were still weak on resin and chlor-alkali prices and on global overcapacity. After the 2025 closures, PEM is a leaner, more Gulf Coast-weighted chlor-vinyls and polyethylene chain.

Consolidated 2025 net sales were $11.170 billion, down from $12.142 billion in 2024 and $12.548 billion in 2023. The 2025 decline was 8 percent: volumes down 5 percent, average prices lower. Gross profit was $813 million, versus $1.957 billion in 2024. GAAP net loss attributable to Westlake was $1.508 billion.

**RM:** How do you want us to read that loss? Identified Items versus the run-rate.

**CFO:** Identified Items in 2025 were $1.392 billion. Facility-shutdown charges in PEM were about $649 million of the adjusted-EBITDA bridge. There was a non-cash goodwill impairment. Goodwill went from $2.031 billion at year-end 2024 to $1.314 billion at year-end 2025. Excluding Identified Items, 2025 net loss attributable was $116 million and EBITDA was $1.144 billion. Reported EBITDA was negative $248 million. Operating cash flow was $465 million. Company-defined free cash flow was negative $530 million. Capex stayed in the $0.9 to $1.1 billion band. The common dividend was maintained through the trough.

The operating issues behind the year were real: PEM overcapacity, lower PVC and polyethylene prices, the Petro 1 ethylene turnaround at Lake Charles, and the plant closures. Do not underwrite 2025 GAAP as the go-forward earnings power, and do not ignore it either.

**RM:** Second quarter looked like a different company. Is that the base you want us to use?

**CFO:** It is a recovery quarter, not a new peak. Second-quarter 2026 net sales were $3.271 billion. Net income attributable was $260 million. EBITDA was $679 million. First-half net sales were $5.923 billion. First-half net income attributable was $91 million — the entire profit print was in the second quarter. First-half income from operations was $192 million: a first-quarter loss of $172 million and second-quarter income of $364 million. First-half free cash flow was negative $192 million, after $416 million of capex, $171 million of acquisitions, and the May note redemption.

PEM EBITDA margin excluding Identified Items expanded to 21 percent in the second quarter from 3 percent a year earlier. Average sales price was 14 percent higher, volume 2 percent higher, ethane and natural-gas costs lower. Polyethylene and PVC resin prices moved up with higher global oil.

The three-pillar profitability plan delivered about $150 million of EBITDA benefit in the quarter and $300 million year-to-date, toward a $600 million target for 2026. The pillars are the closures we have already booked, lower turnaround expense, and cost and reliability actions. S&P’s February note said about $600 million of the 2026 PEM EBITDA improvement is controllable, not a demand rebound. That is how we want it read.

**RM:** HIP guidance, since that is the ballast.

**CFO:** Full-year 2026 HIP revenue guidance is $4.4 to $4.6 billion, EBITDA margin 19 to 21 percent. We expect both at the low end. Residential construction is still muted. Repair-and-remodel and pipe and fittings are why HIP held up through higher mortgage rates. Do not underwrite a housing boom.

**RM:** Balance sheet and the 2026 maturity. That is what the agencies have been watching.

**CFO:** At December 31, 2025: total assets $19.961 billion, cash and cash equivalents $2.724 billion, available-for-sale securities $204 million, face-value borrowings $5.680 billion, current maturities $497 million, long-term debt net $5.087 billion, total equity $9.295 billion of which $503 million is noncontrolling interest, principally Westlake Chemical Partners LP. We own 14.1 million common units, 40.1 percent. That NCI is not parent debt.

At June 30, 2026, after the May 29 redemption of the remaining $496 million of 3.60 percent notes due August 2026: long-term debt net $5.067 billion, principal debt $5.160 billion, no current maturities. Cash and restricted cash on the 10-Q were $1.660 billion. The earnings release cites about $1.9 billion of cash, cash equivalents, and fixed-income investments. Use both figures and do not add them.

Funded debt is almost entirely senior unsecured notes, coupons 1.625 percent to 6.375 percent, maturities 2029 to 2061. In November 2025 we issued $600 million of 5.550 percent notes due 2035 and $600 million of 6.375 percent notes due 2055. Part of that funded a tender of $254 million of the 2026 notes. The balance was redeemed in cash on May 29. Next material maturity is the €700 million 1.625 percent notes due July 2029, carrying value about $805 million at March 31. Tax-exempt waste-disposal bonds are $11 million, immaterial. No subsidiary guarantee on the revolver.

**RM:** Liquidity as you see it, and how you actually use the line.

**Head of Treasury:** June 30 liquidity is about $1.9 billion of cash and fixed-income investments plus the $1.5 billion undrawn revolver, less any letters of credit. LC sublimit is $150 million. We have not been drawing the revolver. Trade receivables were $1.504 billion at year-end and $1.690 billion at March 31. Inventories were $1.653 billion and $1.677 billion. Payables were $783 million and $811 million. Working capital moves with resin price and the building-products season. It does not drive a structural draw. If we needed paper for a short window we would use cash or the swingline before we printed the revolver. That is why tenor and an undrawn backstop matter more to us than size.

**RM:** Two small acquisitions in the first half. How should we treat those?

**CFO:** ACI compounding closed in January 2026, and we bought the Wilhelmshaven PVC and VCM plant. Together about $171 million in the first-half cash flow. Bolt-ons inside the existing chain. Not a change in strategy and not a draw on the revolver. If we did something that changed the leverage path, you would hear it from us before you read it in an 8-K.

**RM:** Family ownership. I have to ask it directly.

**CFO:** The Chao family, through TTWF LP and TTWFGP LLC, beneficially owns about 72.5 percent of the common stock. That is a control overlay. It is not a parental guarantee, and it is not a ratings notch to a holding company. Day-to-day operations sit with Jean-Marc. He is the first non-family chief executive. The Chois remain in the chair roles. Dividend policy has been steady through the trough — S&P’s February update assumed $300 to $350 million of dividends over the next 12 to 24 months. We are not using the revolver to fund a special dividend.

**RM:** Ratings. You are investment grade, but two houses are Negative. How do you want the group to treat that?

**CFO:** S&P downgraded the issuer to BBB from BBB+ on February 18, 2026, Outlook Negative. Moody’s rates the senior unsecured notes Baa2, Outlook Stable, as of the November 2025 issuance. Fitch affirmed BBB on April 15, 2026 and revised the Outlook to Negative. The Negative outlooks are the 2025 trough and the question of whether 2026 EBITDA gets back to the mid- to high-teens hundreds of millions above the $1.144 billion excluding items. Fitch’s April forecast was about $1.8 billion of 2026 EBITDA and year-end leverage about 3.3 times, below 3.0 times in 2027. S&P’s February forecast was about $1.65 billion of S&P-adjusted EBITDA and FFO to debt just over 30 percent. We are not guiding to those numbers. They are the agencies’ numbers. Our number is the $600 million three-pillar target, of which $300 million is already in the first half, and HIP at the low end of the range.

**RM:** Risks you would put on the table yourselves.

**CFO:** PEM price and overcapacity first. A reversal of the second-quarter resin price move puts us back toward the 2025 margin. Ethane and natural gas are a cost offset, not a hedge of the whole chain.

Housing second. HIP is the ballast, and the 2026 guide is already at the low end of the range. A further drop in starts would show up in pipe, siding, and profiles. Repair-and-remodel is stickier than new construction. It is not immune.

Execution on the three-pillar plan. $300 million is in. The other $300 million is still a 2026 target. Closures are booked. The rest is operating performance.

The 2029 euro notes are the next real maturity. Nothing material before that. A shut bond market in 2028 would be a use of the revolver, which is what the revolver is for. We do not need it for a 2026 wall. That wall is gone.

Leadership transition is a fair question. I have been CFO since June. Steve is still advising. Jean-Marc has been CEO for two years. The file is continuous.

**RM:** Anything on seasonality or letters of credit we should put in the memo?

**Head of Treasury:** Building products are seasonal. PEM is a price cycle, not a calendar cycle. Letters of credit are ordinary-course, inside the $150 million sublimit, and they reduce availability. Neither is a reason to upsize the line.

**RM:** Last item. What does a successful hold look like from your chair?

**CFO:** Same $1.5 billion, same April 2, 2031 maturity, same ratings grid, same leverage covenant, undrawn. Pricing that recognizes a BBB / Baa2 name that has taken the 2026 notes out, is inside 3.5 times, and has a published self-help path rather than a hope for 2022 peak resin prices. We will give the group the public file — 2025 10-K, June 30 2026 10-Q, the second-quarter release, the April 6 8-K on the credit agreement, the November 2025 notes prospectus. No forecast beyond what we have guided and what the agencies have already published. If a bank wants Jean-Marc after they have read the file, we will do that. We will not shop a looser structure.

**RM:** That is enough to underwrite. We will come back with a hold recommendation on $1.5 billion, five years to April 2031, senior unsecured, the existing leverage covenant, and mid-grid pricing, mapped to the public file you just walked through. Thank you both.

**CFO:** Treasury will send the filing pack this afternoon.
