# Kinder Morgan, Inc. — Discovery Call Script
**Paste-ready input for LogiCov dev environment**
**Call date:** 8 October 2026
**Duration:** ~30 minutes
**Participants:** Relationship Manager (RM); David P. Michels, Chief Financial Officer, Kinder Morgan, Inc.; Head of Treasury, Kinder Morgan, Inc.
**Purpose of the call:** Discuss the Company’s contracted natural-gas infrastructure position, the May 2026 revolver amendment, the July notes issue, and what the bank group should underwrite on the $3.5 billion senior unsecured facility.

---

## The conversation

**RM:** David, thanks for making time. I have Treasury on as well. This is a discovery conversation, not a term-sheet pitch. We want the company as it sits after the second quarter and the May amendment, and what you want the bank group to do with the revolver. I’ll keep notes and come back with a structure that matches what you tell us today.

**CFO (David Michels):** That is the right frame. Kim Dang is CEO. Rich Kinder is Executive Chairman. Dax Sanders is President. Sital Mody runs Natural Gas Pipelines. The Office of the Chairman is the chief operating decision maker. Underwrite the pipes, not a commodity story.

**RM:** Start with the request so we do not bury it. What do you want from the group?

**Head of Treasury:** The facility is already in place. On May 21, 2026 we entered an Amended and Restated Revolving Credit Agreement with Barclays Bank PLC as administrative agent. It amended and restated the $3.5 billion Revolving Credit Agreement dated August 20, 2021. Same amount. Stated maturity moved from August 20, 2026 to May 21, 2031. Swingline availability moved from $50 million to $400 million. Senior unsecured. Purpose is a liquidity backstop for the commercial paper program, letters of credit, and general corporate purposes. We are not looking to draw it for a project. It was undrawn at December 31, 2025 and at June 30, 2026, and it is still undrawn.

**RM:** Covenant and how the line sits against commercial paper?

**Head of Treasury:** Sole maintenance financial covenant is Consolidated Net Indebtedness to Consolidated EBITDA not more than 5.50 to 1.00. We are well inside it. Company-defined Net Debt to Adjusted EBITDA was 3.6 times at June 30 on a last-twelve-month basis, versus a 2026 budget of 3.8 times. Do not confuse the covenant with the management metric. They are different calculations, and both have headroom.

Commercial paper was $13 million at year-end and $338 million at June 30. Letters of credit were $10 million at both dates. Availability was about $3.477 billion at year-end and about $3.2 billion at June 30, after commercial paper and letters of credit. The revolver is the backstop for the CP program. A draw would be a stress case or a very short window, not the plan.

The drawn-margin grid is ratings-based and sits in the credit agreement. At BBB+ / Baa1 we are at the step that grid assigns to those ratings. We are not asking to reopen pricing.

**RM:** Walk me through the company a lender should underwrite.

**CFO:** We own or operate about 78,000 miles of pipelines, 136 terminals, and about 706 Bcf of working natural gas storage. Four segments. Natural Gas Pipelines is the credit. Products Pipelines and Terminals are fee and tariff businesses with a smaller commodity component. CO2 is residual commodity exposure — enhanced-oil-recovery production, CO2 source and transportation, and a small energy-transition book. Do not underwrite CO2 as the repayment source.

2025 revenue was $16.937 billion, up from $15.100 billion in 2024 and $15.334 billion in 2023. Natural Gas Pipelines was $11.009 billion of that, 65 percent, up from 59 percent in 2024. Products Pipelines $2.686 billion, Terminals $2.104 billion, CO2 $1.170 billion. Segment EBDA — our segment earnings measure before DD&A and corporate — was $8.992 billion. Natural Gas Pipelines was $6.080 billion of that, 68 percent. Products $1.157 billion, Terminals $1.143 billion, CO2 $612 million, about 7 percent.

Net income attributable was $2.391 billion in 2023, $2.613 billion in 2024, $3.056 billion in 2025. Diluted EPS $1.06, $1.17, $1.37. GAAP operating cash flow $6.491 billion, $5.635 billion, $5.917 billion. Cash capex $2.317 billion, $2.629 billion, $3.026 billion. Cash dividends $2.529 billion, $2.557 billion, $2.604 billion.

**RM:** First half and the second quarter. You called the quarter a record.

**CFO:** First-half 2026 revenue was $9.305 billion versus $8.283 billion. Net income attributable $1.843 billion versus $1.432 billion. Diluted EPS $0.82 versus $0.64. Operating cash flow $3.451 billion versus $2.811 billion. Cash capex $1.786 billion versus $1.413 billion.

Second quarter: revenue $4.477 billion. Net income attributable $867 million, a second-quarter record, versus $715 million. Adjusted net income attributable $821 million. Adjusted EPS $0.37. Adjusted EBITDA $2.199 billion. Last-twelve-month adjusted EBITDA $9.000 billion. First-half adjusted EBITDA $4.738 billion. Segment EBDA in the quarter was $2.399 billion, of which Natural Gas Pipelines was $1.520 billion and CO2 was $226 million. The CO2 increase was commodity price and volume. It is not the credit.

Transport volumes were up 7 percent in the quarter. Gathering volumes were up 26 percent. That is contracted and gathered gas, not a bet on the hub price.

**RM:** Budget and what you said after the quarter. I want the guide in your words, separate from anything we extend.

**CFO:** The 2026 budget was net income of $3.1 billion, adjusted EPS of $1.36, adjusted EBITDA of $8.6 billion, and a declared dividend of $1.19 per share. The Board declared $0.2975 per share for each of the first and second quarters, which is the $1.19 annualized. After the second quarter we said we expected to be more than 5 percent favorable to the adjusted-EBITDA budget and more than 12 percent favorable to the adjusted-EPS budget, with year-end Net Debt to Adjusted EBITDA of 3.6 times versus the 3.8 times budget. That is the Company update. We have not published a 2027 or 2028 budget. If a bank builds a phased backlog case past 2026, label it as the bank’s case.

**RM:** Backlog. This is the growth story. How hard should we lean on it?

**CFO:** At June 30 the project backlog was $9.6 billion. Ninety-two percent natural gas. More than 60 percent tied to power generation and local distribution. Project multiple about 5.6 times. The existing system is in service and is the repayment source. Backlog EBITDA is upside. Interstate expansions need FERC certificates. A delay on Mississippi Crossing, South System Expansion 4, or Trident would defer EBITDA. It would not impair the cash flow that services this revolver. Projects already in service in the second quarter reduce the unpermitted share. We are not asking the banks to underwrite unpermitted EBITDA as the grade.

**RM:** Balance sheet, the 2026 and 2027 notes, and the July issue.

**CFO:** Cash is thin by design. $63 million at year-end 2025, $89 million at June 30. We run a CP program against the revolver rather than a cash pile. Current portion of debt was $1.226 billion at year-end and $2.443 billion at June 30. Long-term debt outstanding was $30.597 billion at year-end and $29.701 billion at June 30. Total assets $72.748 billion and $74.062 billion. Total equity $32.449 billion and $32.879 billion, including noncontrolling interests.

The June 30 current portion includes the 4.15 percent notes due August 2026, the 1.75 percent notes due November 2026, and early-2027 maturities. On May 1, 2025 we issued $1.100 billion of 5.15 percent notes due 2030 and $750 million of 5.85 percent notes due 2035. Combined net proceeds $1.834 billion.

On July 28 we priced $1.150 billion of 5.550 percent senior notes due August 1, 2036 and $600 million of 6.150 percent senior notes due August 1, 2056. Settlement was August 6, after the 10-Q balance sheet, so those proceeds are not in the June 30 cash number. Joint bookrunners included BofA Securities, CIBC, PNC, RBC, and Wells Fargo. That issue is the takeout path for the near-term notes. A shut bond market would push residual maturities onto CP and, if CP cleared badly, onto the revolver. That is what the revolver is for. The July print says the bond market is open at these ratings.

**RM:** Ratings. You are aligned now. How do you want the group to treat the path?

**CFO:** S&P upgraded the long-term rating to BBB+ from BBB on January 13, 2026. The 2025 10-K still showed BBB / Positive at year-end, which was correct as of that date. Moody’s upgraded the senior unsecured rating to Baa1 from Baa2 on March 12, 2026, Outlook Stable. We said that on the first-quarter release. Fitch is BBB+ / Stable, as listed on the July 28 term sheet. The term sheet’s expected ratings on the new notes were Moody’s Baa1, S&P BBB+, and Fitch BBB+, each Stable. We are not asking for a notch above the agencies. The trajectory that got us here was leverage at the low end of the range and contracted gas infrastructure, not a commodity spike.

**RM:** Competitive set, so we do not underwrite you against a producer.

**CFO:** The relevant names are other North American energy infrastructure credits. Williams is the closest large gas-pipeline comparable — S&P BBB+ / Stable as of April 2026, Moody’s Baa2 / Positive on their own disclosure. ONEOK is the gas-gathering and NGL peer, Moody’s Baa2 / Stable as of August 2026. Enbridge is the larger North American infrastructure credit, senior unsecured BBB+ / Baa2, and it reports in Canadian dollars, so do not drop it into a dollar table without a conversion. Our differentiation is the contracted natural-gas backlog tied to power and LDC load, the 3.6 times net-debt metric, and a revolver that is a CP backstop rather than a working-capital line for a commodity book.

**RM:** Risks you would put on the table yourselves.

**CFO:** Refinancing of the 2026 and 2027 notes first. Current debt was $2.443 billion at June 30. The July issue addresses it. Until those proceeds are applied, it is a liquidity use, not a solvency issue.

Volume and contract recontracting second. Transport and gathering were up in the quarter. A sustained drop in producer activity would show up in gathering before it showed up in long-haul transport. The long-haul book is the ballast.

Regulatory and permit risk on the backlog. I already said it. Delay is an EBITDA deferral. The in-service system pays the dividend and the interest.

CO2 and products margins. CO2 was 7 percent of 2025 revenue and about 7 percent of segment EBDA. Products earnings move with refined-product margins. Treat both as residual. Natural Gas Pipelines was 65 percent of revenue and 68 percent of segment EBDA.

Dividend and capex together. We paid $2.604 billion of dividends in 2025 against $5.917 billion of operating cash flow and $3.026 billion of cash capex. The $1.19 dividend is the published policy. We are not using the revolver to fund it. If backlog spend and the dividend ever competed for liquidity, the revolver is the bridge, not the plan.

**RM:** Anything on letters of credit or day-to-day use we should put in the memo?

**Head of Treasury:** Letters of credit are ordinary-course, $10 million at the last two balance-sheet dates. CP is the day-to-day funding tool, $338 million at June 30, inside the backstop. Swingline at $400 million is for same-day cash management, not a project draw. We do not seasonally draw the revolver.

**RM:** Last item. What does a successful hold look like from your chair?

**CFO:** Same $3.5 billion, same May 21, 2031 maturity, same 5.50 times covenant, swingline at $400 million, undrawn except for the CP backstop mechanic. Pricing that recognizes a BBB+ / Baa1 / BBB+ name with leverage at 3.6 times and a bond market that just took 10-year and 30-year paper. We will give the group the public file — 2025 10-K, June 30 2026 10-Q, the July 22 earnings release, the May 28 8-K on the amendment, the July 28 pricing term sheet. No forecast beyond the budget and the post-quarter update. If a bank wants Kim after they have read the file, we will do that. We will not shop a looser covenant.

**RM:** That is enough to underwrite. We will come back with a hold recommendation on $3.5 billion, maturity May 21, 2031, senior unsecured, the 5.50 times covenant, and ratings-grid pricing, mapped to the public file you just walked through. Thank you both.

**CFO:** Treasury will send the filing pack this afternoon.
